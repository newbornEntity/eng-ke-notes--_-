## Theme and Introduction

The poem presents **ageing, filial love, the fear of separation, mortality, and the emotional restraint of parting**. During a drive to Cochin airport, the speaker suddenly recognises her mother's frailty. She tries to push the thought away by looking at signs of youth and movement outside, but the same fear returns at the airport. Her repeated smile hides pain while reassuring her mother.

The poem's power comes from understatement. The speaker never delivers a dramatic farewell. Instead, ordinary observations—sleeping in a car, looking through a window, standing after security check—carry the emotional weight of possible loss.

## Poet

**Kamala Das (1934-2009)** was a major Indian poet and writer from Kerala. She wrote in English and Malayalam, using the Malayalam pen name **Madhavikutty**. Her writing is known for emotional honesty, lyrical directness, and close attention to complicated human relationships. This poem transforms an intimate mother-daughter moment into a universal experience.

## Central Idea

Awareness of a parent's ageing revives the speaker's childhood fear of losing her mother. Although ageing and separation cannot be prevented, love expresses itself through attention, controlled emotion, and a hopeful smile. The poem therefore balances **private anguish with public composure**.

## Movement-wise Explanation

> **Form note:** NCERT points out that the entire poem is one sentence joined by commas. It has no conventional stanza breaks, so the explanation below follows its emotional movements.

### Movement 1: The journey and sudden recognition

The speaker is driving from her parental home to Cochin airport. Her mother sits beside her, sleeping with her mouth open. The mother's pale, slack appearance suddenly makes the speaker confront her age and physical weakness. The comparison with a lifeless body does not announce actual death; it reveals the daughter's immediate fear of mortality.

### Movement 2: Deliberate distraction

Unable to remain with that painful thought, the speaker looks outside. Trees seem to race backward because the car is moving, while happy children rush out of their homes. Speed, youth, activity, and play contrast sharply with the still, ageing mother inside the car. The outside scene does not erase fear; it is an attempted emotional escape.

### Movement 3: Distance after security check

At the airport, physical distance now separates mother and daughter. Looking again from a few yards away, the speaker sees her mother as pale and weak, like a moon late in winter. The comparison suggests reduced brightness, coldness, and the final phase of a natural cycle. Unlike the shocking earlier comparison, this image is tender and controlled.

### Movement 4: Childhood fear and the masking smile

The sight revives an old, familiar ache—the fear of losing a parent that has existed since childhood. The speaker does not voice this fear. She says that they will meet again and repeatedly smiles. The smile serves three purposes: it reassures the mother, maintains normalcy at departure, and conceals the daughter's pain from both of them.

## Poetic Devices and Where They Occur

| Device | Where it occurs | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Simile** | Mother's face described as **“ashen like … a corpse”** in the car | Conveys pallor and the daughter's sudden fear of mortality. |
| **Simile** | Mother compared to a **“late winter's moon”** at the airport | Suggests fading vitality, coldness, age, and the closing part of a life cycle. |
| **Personification** | **“Young Trees sprinting”** outside the moving car | Makes the world appear energetic and youthful; also captures the optical effect of motion. |
| **Metaphorical verb** | Children are shown “spilling” out of homes | Suggests abundance, spontaneity, and uncontrolled youthful energy. |
| **Contrast / juxtaposition** | Ageing mother inside versus trees and children outside | Places stillness and decline against youth, movement, and continuity of life. |
| **Symbolism** | Late-winter moon | Winter suggests decline; the dim moon symbolises weakened life and approaching separation. |
| **Repetition** | **“smile and smile and smile”** at the end | Emphasises sustained emotional control and an effort to cover fear with reassurance. |
| **Enjambment** | Thought runs across nearly every line without full stops | Reproduces the continuous flow of perception and anxiety. |
| **Single-sentence structure** | Whole poem linked through commas | Joins present observation, suppressed thought, memory, and farewell as one mental stream. |
| **Visual imagery** | Pale face, moving trees, children, distant mother | Makes emotional contrasts visible rather than explaining them abstractly. |
| **Free verse** | No fixed rhyme scheme or regular metre | Creates the natural movement of memory and conversation. |
| **Direct address** | The farewell to “Amma” | Adds intimacy and returns the universal fear to a personal relationship. |

## Main Points

1. The speaker notices her sixty-six-year-old mother sleeping beside her during a drive.
2. The mother's pale appearance creates a sudden awareness of ageing and possible separation.
3. The speaker looks outside to avoid the thought.
4. Racing trees and cheerful children symbolise youth, vitality, and life's continuity.
5. At the airport, distance makes the mother's frailty visible again.
6. The winter-moon image is gentler but confirms decline.
7. The moment revives a fear carried since childhood.
8. The speaker hides pain behind hopeful words and repeated smiles.
9. The poem is one sentence, mirroring a continuous emotional current.
10. Love appears through observation and restraint rather than dramatic declaration.

## Summary

While travelling to Cochin airport, Kamala Das observes her ageing mother asleep beside her and is struck by her pallor and weakness. To avoid thinking about loss, she looks at youthful trees and children outside. After the airport security check, she again notices her mother's faded appearance and feels the familiar childhood fear of separation. She suppresses the pain, offers a hopeful farewell, and smiles repeatedly. The poem captures the universal difficulty of accepting a parent's ageing while trying to protect that parent from one's own fear.

## Board Exam Focus

### Extract hotspots

- The mother's sleeping face and the first simile.
- Trees and children as contrast.
- The airport view and winter-moon image.
- The final farewell and repeated smile.

### High-value analytical angles

- How do the two similes differ in tone and purpose?
- Why does the speaker look outside, and why does the distraction fail?
- How do movement and stillness structure the poem?
- What does the repeated smile conceal and communicate?
- Why is the childhood fear described as old and familiar?
- How does the single-sentence form reflect the speaker's mind?

### Common mistakes

- The mother is asleep and frail; the poem does not state that she is dead.
- The trees do not literally run—the apparent motion comes from the moving car.
- The children are not random scenery; they create the central contrast with ageing.
- The smile is not proof of happiness. It is reassurance, hope, and emotional concealment.

### One-line revision core

**An adult daughter's childhood fear returns when her mother's ageing becomes suddenly visible, and she masks that fear with a hopeful smile.**
